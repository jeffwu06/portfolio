Project 01

Model Rocket

The goal of this project was to design and launch an APCP (ammonium perchlorate composite propellant) based model rocket. This rocket would have an avionics bay and an automatic parachute deployment system.

Major skills

OpenRocket - Rocket Design, Flight Simulations OnShape - CAD Manufacturing - 3D Printing, Laser Cutting Documentation & Research

Project Gallery

Process

Numerous designs were first analyzed on OpenRocket. Features such as the rocket’s fin, head shape and body length were all considered. Since we wanted to minimize the rocket’s mass and maximize the altitude, we tested various different designs until we landed on one that not only had a high altitude (500m), but also lightweight (1.2kg).

OpenRocket’s simulation software was then used to test various weather conditions for our rocket. We found that although our rocket had a high altitude and was lightweight, it was heavily affected by wind. Subsequently, a redesign was done by changing the nose cone and fins, allowing the rocket to now also fly more straight.

Next, our rocket design from OpenRocket was recreated in OnShape. Individual components such the nose cone had to be specifically designed as it would house the avionics bay and parachute.

With our CAD done, we moved onto the manufacturing process. The body and fins were cut from a hardboard tube using laser cutting, whereas the cone was 3D printed. Once all pieces were printed, we used epoxy to connect each piece together. Lastly, the motor, avionics, and parachute were all installed on launch day. Note that the avionics systems were preprogrammed, but the purpose of it was to read values such as RSSI, quaternions, air pressure, and deploy the parachute.

Outcome

On launch day, the weather conditions were very windy. However, our rocket successfully launched and showed an apogee of 442m. Post launch, a post flight report was written and aimed to document the entire design and manufacturing process along with the post flight data.
